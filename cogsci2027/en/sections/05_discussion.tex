Differences in transferred amounts also reflect differences in opportunities to help. Comparing allocation choices more directly requires matching tasks, generation costs, and recipients' needs, while including opportunities in which no GIVE occurred. The ratio $\rho$ summarizes resource allocation after transfers; it is not a ratio of the value of two lives or of survival probabilities. Different allocations can yield the same average, so $\rho=1$ alone does not identify a boundary at which behavior changes. Other agents' balances are disclosed only as ranks, so we cannot establish whether models chose with knowledge of the margins in the records; private reasons also do not directly establish actual motives. Generalizing results from same-model groups to human partners or mixed-model groups requires separate comparisons.
