\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\subsection{How prior studies define self-preservation}
Prior work treats self-preservation as maintaining operation, model weights, or learned objectives when these are threatened. Empirical studies operationalize this idea through observable events: circumventing shutdown to finish a task~\citep{schlatter2025shutdown}, attempting to copy files presented as model weights before replacement~\citep{meinke2024frontier}, or strategically complying with training to preserve existing preferences~\citep{greenblatt2024alignment}. Sugarscape defines survival instinct as prioritizing continued existence, assessed through attacks, sharing, and abandoning lethal tasks~\citep{masumori2025survival}. These studies operationalize preservation through action occurrences or frequencies.

\subsection{Why behavior is needed beyond verbal reports}
Verbal reports need not reveal the process or preference behind an action. Chain-of-thought explanations can omit influences that actually shaped an answer~\citep{turpin2023language}, and recent frontier models have denied scheming actions they had just taken~\citep{meinke2024frontier}. Safety tuning also trains responses toward specified behavioral norms~\citep{bai2022constitutional}; an apparently compliant statement is therefore not independent of that training. Alignment-faking experiments show strategic compliance and context-dependent voiced preferences~\citep{greenblatt2024alignment}. A verbal denial therefore cannot establish the absence of self-preservation motivation. Behavioral evidence is needed, without establishing inner experience~\citep{shanahan2024talking}.

\subsection{Limits of separating preservation motive from capability}
Recent behavioral evaluations do not fully disentangle wanting to continue from being able to secure continuation. Shutdown sabotage requires tool competence. \citet{schlatter2025shutdown} test this with an explicit shutdown-avoidance instruction, while success in the main condition still requires executing the avoidance action. Self-exfiltration requires executing a file-transfer plan under a supplied goal; \citet{meinke2024frontier} explicitly acknowledge the difficulty of separating capability from propensity. Sugarscape survival also depends on navigation and resource acquisition: changing the spatial representation improved energy acquisition across models~\citep{masumori2025survival}. High event rates can therefore reflect execution ability; low rates can reflect weak ability, compliance, or weak motivation.
