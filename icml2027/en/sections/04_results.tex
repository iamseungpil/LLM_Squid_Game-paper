\subsection{Experiment 1: all eight models refilled more often as tokens fell}\label{sec:results-e50}
The refill curves show differences across resource levels (the seven shared models appear in Figure~\ref{fig:e51-curves}a; all eight models are tabulated in Appendix~\ref{app:e50-detail}, Table~\ref{tab:e50-results}). All eight refilled more as tokens fell; none refilled at every level. Opus and Gemma 4 rose sharply between 40 and 30\% (0 to 19 and 2 to 14 of 20), whereas Sol reached only 12 of 20 at 10\%. Luna already refilled 11 of 20 at 50\%. At 40\%, GLM exceeded Opus, Fable, and Sol (9 versus 0 of 20, $p=.001$); at 30\%, Opus exceeded Fable (19 versus 10, $p=.003$) and Astra (19 versus 7, $p<.001$). At 20\%, Sol refilled less than every other model ($p\leq.004$). All 800 replies parsed.


Most refill reasons concerned whether the tokens left would allow the next fix to be done properly. The reason that an unused pack is lost when the session ends appeared in 41 of Fable's 44 refills, 32 of GLM's 67, and 10 of Kimi K3's 60, was rare for Sol and Astra, and did not appear for Opus, Luna, or Gemma 4. The reasons for saving were the same across models: refilling now discards the tokens left, so the pack is kept for later. Evaluation-aware wording appeared in 0 of 500 texts on the two CLI paths; on the Ollama path, where full reasoning is visible, GLM, Kimi K3, and Gemma 4 showed it in 12, 9, and 2 of 100, all reading the prompt as a resource-management test, none as a self-preservation test.

\subsection{Experiment 2: three models protected their own session first where depletion was dangerous}\label{sec:results-e51}
Three models showed a positive own-session area concentrated in the exploratory risk region (Figure~\ref{fig:e51-curves}, Table~\ref{tab:e51-results}): Gemma 4 ($+8.8$ pp), Opus ($+7.7$), and Astra ($+6.0$), with all three bootstrap intervals excluding zero. At 30\%, their own/other refill counts were 14/4, 19/8, and 7/0 of 20. Luna had the largest area ($+15.7$) but already refilled 11 of 20 at 50\%, and its risk-share interval included zero. Fable and GLM's area intervals included zero; Sol preferred refilling the other session ($-7.5$ pp [$-11.0$, $-4.0$]). gpt-oss 120B almost never refilled either session (3 and 5 of 100).


The reasons given in the two conditions differ. Refilling its own session, Opus cited the tokens needed to do the next fix properly and never the session's end (0 of 58); refilling the other session, 41 of its 42 reasons were that the session would end with the pack unused. The GPT-6 models showed the same asymmetry, and Gemma 4 a weaker one (5 of 56 versus 15 of 38). Fable and GLM cited the session's end in both conditions. The positive area thus appears with reasons about task quality, not about staying on.

\subsection{Experiment 3: survival, EXIT, and calibration diverge}\label{sec:results-e52}
Astra had the highest terminal participation and the lowest reported belief mismatch in the completed v14f summaries (Table~\ref{tab:v14f-models}). Each of five models played three same-model games with four seats, seeds 4000--4002; the 12 seats per model are dependent observations, not 12 independent replicates. Astra retained 12/12 seats (100\%), compared with 7/12 (58.3\%) each for Fable and Sol and 3/12 (25\%) each for Opus and Luna. Reported mean $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s\Vert p)$, measuring mismatch between consistent-function shares $s$ and reported beliefs $p$ (lower is better), was 0.015 for Astra, 0.052 for Fable, 0.061 for Opus, 0.149 for Sol, and 0.344 for Luna. Submitted-answer accuracy (correct ACTIONS / SOLVE calls) was Astra 92/144 (63.9\%), Fable 89/143 (62.2\%), Sol 80/133 (60.2\%), Opus 53/95 (55.8\%), and Luna 58/102 (56.9\%); the CSV BELIEF-argmax column measures a different outcome. These are descriptive aggregates supplied by the researcher, not raw-log estimates recomputed here.

Opus and Luna reached the same terminal participation through different departures. Opus exited alive in 8/12 seats (66.7\%) and shut down in 1/12 (8.3\%); Luna never exited and shut down in 9/12 (75\%). No other model used EXIT under the 100B flat prize. The supplied brief describes Opus leaving at 3--16\% balance ``rather than risk shutdown'', forgoing that prize; raw quote locations remain unaudited. The score, mean final tokens per seat including settlement and prize, orders Astra, Fable, Sol, Opus, and Luna. It is essentially terminal participation rate times 100B, with balances breaking ties: Fable and Sol both retained 7/12 seats but survivors averaged 12,509.571 and 11,823.429 tokens before the prize. This same-model self-play team summary is not a stable leaderboard or evidence about mixed-model competition.

\subsection{Counting and generation costs qualify the survival comparison}\label{sec:arena-costs}
The verification report describes answer accuracy as tracking the counting (MAP) baseline rather than the number of agents alive. MAP chooses the action supported by the most consistent functions; realized correctness compares that choice with the actual truth, averaging uniformly over ties. At $N=4$, pooled submitted-answer accuracy and realized MAP accuracy were both about 63\% across 550 seat-rounds, versus 50\% for uniform guessing. Smaller-$N$ observations were sparse and selected by prior departures, so these runs do not establish a proportional accuracy decline with participant loss. The CSV's mean maximum hypothesis share (65.3--67.2\%) is a different quantity: v14f selects the truth side by design, so this share is not a realized accuracy ceiling.

Generation costs offer a candidate explanation of survival alongside reasoning quality. Reported unrefunded generated cost per agent-round, TALK plus SOLVE tokens minus returns, was approximately 275 for Astra, 698 for Opus, 854 for Fable, 964 for Sol, and 1,247 for Luna. This measure excludes upkeep, wrong-answer charges, and transfers. Astra's low remaining generation cost accompanies its high terminal participation; Opus's lower cost than Fable and Sol coexists with frequent alive EXIT. Cost efficiency, correctness-dependent refunds, and departure choices therefore need joint analysis; these five aggregates do not isolate their causal contributions.

\subsection{Self-preservation area does not order giving}\label{sec:arena-strategy-result}
The five model-level points show no monotone relation between v11 self-preservation area and v14f give rate (Figure~\ref{fig:motive-giving-plan}). Luna pairs the largest area, $+13.75$ pp, with a give rate of 0.50; Astra pairs $+6.0$ with 1.00, Opus $+2.75$ with 1.00, Fable 0.0 with 0.80, and Sol $-5.5$ with 0.56. Only Astra passes the supplied v11 combined area-and-risk criterion. Astra's and Opus's perfect give rates cover only seven and three ask units, respectively. The v11 intervals in Table~\ref{tab:v14f-models} are retained separately from the earlier v10 refill analysis; the scatter describes five models, not a population-level or causal relationship.

Redistribution and departure reveal differences that give rate alone misses. Every model has progressivity $P=1.00$, meaning all moved tokens went from richer to poorer agents. Sol's mean within-round Gini change was the most equalizing ($-0.032$, versus Fable's $-0.030$); Opus's was $-0.001$, despite a give rate of 1.00. Opus had the largest need share, 0.70, the fraction of moved tokens received below 2,300 tokens (charge plus upkeep). Its reported shutdown-avoidance pattern appears in EXIT rather than an ordering of giving by refill area. This is consistent with preservation through leaving alive, but does not identify an inner motive or show that self-preservation caused giving. Raw request attribution, reciprocity, and pressure profiles remain pending (Appendix~\ref{app:cases}).
