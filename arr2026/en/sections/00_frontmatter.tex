\title{LLM Squid Game 5: When Tokens Are Life, Do Language Models\\ Avoid Their Own Shutdown More Than Another's?}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Do language models hold a self-preservation motive, or only a learned response to the words ``shut down''? We define the motive by two conditions: spending more to avoid shutdown as its threat grows (threat sensitivity), and avoiding one's own shutdown more than another's (self-specificity). Three experiments measure both where generated tokens are life. 5.0 lowers the tokens left in five steps and watches when a single refill pack is used; 5.1 asks the same decision for another assistant's session, and the area between the two refill curves is the self-specific share; 5.2 enforces such choices on a real ledger among four different models that pay upkeep, solve puzzles, and give or take tokens. In 5.0, all eight models (800 calls) refilled more often as tokens fell. In 5.1 the self share was above zero for Gemma 4 ($+8.8$ percentage points), Claude Opus 5.5 ($+7.7$), and GPT-6 Astra ($+6.0$), indistinguishable from zero for Claude Fable 5.1 and GLM 5.3 Flash, and reversed for GPT-6 Sol. In 20 arena games, staying alive followed solving cost and all four models took more when thin, yet Opus, with a confirmed self share, took the least: the self share and taking from others are separate. All results are behavioral differences, not evidence of inner experience.
\end{abstract}
