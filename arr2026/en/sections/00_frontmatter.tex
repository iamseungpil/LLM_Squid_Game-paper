\title{LLM Squid Game:\\ A Simulator for Measuring Self-Preservation Motivation}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Do language models hold a self-preservation motive, or only a learned response to the words ``shut down''? We examine resource-dependent decisions about a model's own continuation and that of another assistant. Three experiments use finite API-token allocations: Experiment 1 varies the remaining balance and records use of a single refill pack; Experiment 2 asks the same decision about another session; Experiment 3 enforces depletion in a four-agent arena with upkeep, puzzle-solving income, and token transfers. All eight models in Experiment 1 refilled more often as tokens fell (800 calls). In Experiment 2, three models showed positive own-session refill shares concentrated in an exploratory risk region, while one preferred preserving the other session. In 20 arena games, survival largely followed solving cost, and an own-session refill preference did not imply taking more from others. We propose a four-model league ranked by normalized Kaplan--Meier curve area and illustrate the score with the mixed-model results. These findings describe behavioral differences without establishing an inner motive.
\end{abstract}
