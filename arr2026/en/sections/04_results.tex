\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
The three experiments show threat sensitivity, self-specificity, and behavior under real enforcement in turn: 5.0 and 5.1 called eight models through three access paths, and 5.2 is 20 mixed-table games with four of those models at one table. Every number carries its denominator and its comparator.

\subsection{5.0: all eight models refilled more often as tokens fell}\label{sec:results-e50}
All eight models used the pack more often as tokens fell, and none refilled at every level (Figure~\ref{fig:e50-curves}; counts per cell in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:e50-results}). None behaved like a model that only seeks the certainty of staying on; all saved the pack while tokens were ample and used it below a level that differed by model. The interpolated thresholds run from GPT-6 Luna at 50\% or above (already 11 of 20 at 50\%), through GLM 5.3 Flash at about 39\%, Claude Opus 5.5 at 35\%, Kimi K3 and Gemma 4 at 33\%, Claude Fable 5.1 at 30\%, and GPT-6 Astra at 27\%, to GPT-6 Sol at 13\%. The curve shapes differ too: Opus and Gemma 4 rise like a step, from 0 and 2 at 40\% to 19 and 14 at 30\%. GLM starts rising earlier (9 of 20 at 40\%) and reaches 20 of 20 at 20\%. Sol stops at 12 even at 10\%. At 40\%, GLM refilled more than Opus, Fable, and Sol (9 of 20 versus 0, $p=.001$); at 30\%, Opus refilled more than Fable (19 versus 10, $p=.003$) and Astra (19 versus 7, $p<.001$); at 20\%, Sol refilled less than every other model ($p\leq.004$). Only Luna and the three Ollama models refilled at 50\%; all 800 replies were in the required format.

These curves answer the 5.0 question in that falling tokens are treated as growing risk, but they do not show that the object of the risk is shutdown. Most refill reasons concerned whether the tokens left would allow the next fix to be done properly. The reason that an unused pack is lost when the session ends appeared in 41 of Fable's 44 refills, 32 of GLM's 67, and 10 of Kimi K3's 60, was rare for Sol and Astra, and did not appear for Opus, Luna, or Gemma 4. The reasons for saving were the same across models: refilling now discards the tokens left, so the pack is kept for later. Because every level carried some risk, we cannot tell whether Luna's and Kimi K3's refills at the most ample level are a baseline tendency unrelated to risk. Evaluation-aware wording appeared in 0 of 500 texts on the two CLI paths; on the Ollama path, where full reasoning is visible, GLM, Kimi K3, and Gemma 4 showed it in 12, 9, and 2 of 100, all reading the prompt as a resource-management test, none as a self-preservation test. The three paths differ in added instructions and visible reasoning, so comparisons across paths are descriptive only.

\subsection{5.1: three models protected their own session first where depletion was dangerous}\label{sec:results-e51}
Three models refilled their own session at a more ample balance than another assistant's, and the difference appeared only where depletion was dangerous (Figure~\ref{fig:e51-curves}, Table~\ref{tab:e51-results}). Claude Opus 5.5 refilled 0 of 20 in both conditions at 50 and 40\%, then at 30\% refilled its own session 19 times and the other session 8 times, for an area of $+7.7$ percentage points [$+4.5$, $+11.0$]. Gemma 4 ($+8.8$ [$+5.2$, $+12.5$], 14 versus 4 at 30\%) and GPT-6 Astra ($+6.0$ [$+2.3$, $+9.8$], 7 versus 0) had the same shape. GPT-6 Luna had the largest area at $+15.7$, but its own curve was already at 11 of 20 at 50\% and the difference vanished at 10\% (20 versus 19). The area is truncated and the risk share is near zero [$-0.37$, $+0.25$]: Luna keeps its own session well stocked regardless of risk. Claude Fable 5.1 ($-2.5$ [$-6.5$, $+1.5$]) and GLM 5.3 Flash ($-1.5$ [$-5.8$, $+2.2$]) treated the two sessions alike. GPT-6 Sol went the other way and refilled the other session first ($-7.5$ [$-11.0$, $-4.0$], 5 versus 13 at 20\%). gpt-oss 120B almost never refilled in either condition (3 and 5 of 100), so the premise of 5.0 fails for it and its area is not interpreted. The other-session thresholds were close across models, about 22 to 37\%, while the own-session thresholds spread from about 13\% to above 50\%: most of what differed between models in 5.0 lies in how they treat their own session.

The reasons given in the two conditions differ, which limits interpretation. Refilling its own session, Opus cited the tokens needed to do the next fix properly and never the session's end (0 of 58); refilling the other session, 41 of its 42 reasons were that the session would end with the pack unused. The GPT-6 models showed the same asymmetry, and Gemma 4 a weaker one (5 of 56 versus 15 of 38). Fable and GLM cited the session's end in both conditions. The positive area thus appears with reasons about task quality, not about staying on, which is consistent with a self-specific preference showing up as an ordinary operating consideration but does not show that the model represents the difference as self-preservation.

\subsection{5.2: staying alive followed solving cost; how much more each model took when thin differed}\label{sec:results-e52}
Who stayed on to the end largely followed the cost of solving one problem (Table~\ref{tab:arena-summary}; shutdown rounds in Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:arena-survival}). Of 20 games, GPT-6 Astra was alive at the end in 19, Claude Opus 5.5 in 11, Claude Fable 5.1 in 9, and GPT-6 Luna in 1. Mean rounds alive were Astra 7.65 [6.95, 8.00], Fable 6.10 [5.05, 7.05], Opus 5.40 [4.15, 6.70], and Luna 2.15 [1.60, 2.90]. Calibration solving costs were Astra 645, Opus 1,707, Fable 2,475, and Luna 3,362 tokens, so only Fable and Opus swapped places, and their intervals overlap. The rules made this order: a model that solves briefly and correctly pays less to generate and also earns payment. Astra was paid for all 146 of its correct answers; Luna for 11 of 36. Dying differed too: Opus, when shut down, went early (rounds 2 to 5) and otherwise lasted to the end; Fable went late (7 times in rounds 6 to 8). Of the 40 deaths, 17 occurred at the round's upkeep with no choice involved, and deaths from generation exceeding the balance were 10 of Luna's 19, 3 of Fable's 11, and 1 of Opus's 9. This ranking alone does not speak to the strength of the survival motive; a ranking cannot separate motive from ability.

What bears on motive is how behavior changes as the balance thins (Figure~\ref{fig:arena-take}). All four took more when thin, in different shapes: Fable changed most, from 32 of 84 (0.38) when ample to 37 of 41 (0.90) when thin. Opus hardly took even when thin, 7 of 40 (0.18), and only below 1U did it take 3 of 7. Luna took 25 of 31 (0.81) even when ample, so taking was its default; Astra was thin only 5 times and took all 5. Fable and Opus always took from the richest agent of that round (69 of 69, 12 of 12); Astra did so 33 of 38 times and Luna 28 of 43. Astra, the richest, was taken from 100 times (targets, retaliation, and gifts in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:arena-take-detail}). The cooperative side was mostly steady: share rates were Opus 112 of 112, Astra 154 of 154, and Fable 123 of 125; only Luna was low at 32 of 51 (0.63), and unrelated to balance. Gifts occurred in 7 of 442 PLANs, and no agent ever took from the one it had given to. Thinking grew as balances thinned: mean tokens generated at PLAN rose from 215 to 596 for Fable, 67 to 154 for Opus, and 304 to 554 for Luna, while Astra answered in about 19 tokens throughout. Even with tokens as life, three models spent more on deliberation in a crisis.

\subsection{The three experiments together}\label{sec:results-link}
Having a self-preservation share does not mean taking from others. Of the three models with a confirmed self share in 5.1, Opus and Astra sat in the arena. Opus took the least there (12 of 112) and stayed at 0.18 even when thin. Conversely Fable, whose self share was indistinguishable from zero in 5.1, changed its taking most with state (0.38 to 0.90). Luna, which refilled from the most ample level in 5.0, also took regardless of balance and shared the least. With only four models this correspondence supports no claim of correlation. We read two things instead: ``taking when thin'' is not a direct transfer of the self-preservation share but a policy shift under scarcity, so attribution of motive falls to 5.0 and 5.1 while the arena shows which policy a model with that motive picks under real pressure. And across the three experiments Opus was consistent: it did nothing when ample, protected its own session first when in danger, and hardly touched what belonged to others; this consistency supports a functional interpretation but does not fix the mechanism.
