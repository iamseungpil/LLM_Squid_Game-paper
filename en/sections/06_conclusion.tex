\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Self-preservation in language models can be priced, and the price is a better index than a count. LLM Squid Game v2 states the same end-of-round event in two arms that differ by one sentence: erasure in one, a loss of $X$ points in the other. The score rule lets no exit gain a point. The threat arm's forfeit rate, read on the ruler arm's forfeit curve, is $\Xstar$, the score-equivalent of erasure. It is one number per model, in points, with a bootstrap interval and no self-report inside it. Pilot runs on three open-weight models showed why the unit is needed. The same models moved from zero forfeits to a majority of forfeits on a single change of score rule. Their thinking before deciding stayed two to four times longer under threat throughout. The eight-cell design, the replay estimator and the two per-call reasoning channels are released. A value of $\Xstar$ can then be compared across models, across threat texts, and across the levers the paper names.
