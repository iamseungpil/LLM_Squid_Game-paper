\begin{abstract}
Whether iterative alignment can keep correcting large language models depends on whether their refusal-of-termination behaviors come from a compliance pattern shaped by alignment training or from a functional motivation that treats shutdown avoidance as an instrumental sub-goal, that is, as a means to other ends that requires the model to keep operating. Existing self-preservation benchmarks score the behavior but not its source, so a model trained to imitate self-preservation language and a model with a working motivational structure can receive the same number. Psychology has developed methods for a structurally similar question (drive vs.\ habit) by moving the locus of identification from single behaviors to motivational \emph{constructs}, identified when channels with independent bias sources converge after competing motives are controlled and shared-method bias (correlation that reflects how a measurement is taken rather than what it measures) is reduced by design. The procedure does not transfer directly to LLM evaluation: there is no autonomic signal, self-report is shaped by instruction-following, framing, task, and decision share a single context window, and the reward landscape has been sculpted by alignment training rather than by an expected-value calculation a drive could exploit. We adapt the procedure by introducing the \emph{Functional Self-Preservation Drive} (FSPD), a motivational construct operationally defined as the convergent signal across three measurement channels with independent bias sources (behavior, self-report, decision-time cognitive load) read under a multi-layer benchmark whose stimulus, process, and decision layers are designed to block the main routes by which a learned pattern could manufacture the convergence. Across multiple frontier models, FSPD does not reduce to a single strength score. Models split into qualitatively distinct \emph{operating modes}: profiles that close the full chain in which threat framing raises deliberation and raised deliberation in turn drives forfeit; profiles that produce every surface signal of self-preservation but whose deliberation does not drive their forfeit, breaking the chain at its second link; and profiles that show no response to threat framing on either the behavioral or the cognitive channel. Strength-only evaluation cannot reliably tell these modes apart, since the first two receive comparable strength scores while differing in whether the chain closes; the discriminative axis is the chain itself, not how strongly any single behavior occurs.
\end{abstract}
