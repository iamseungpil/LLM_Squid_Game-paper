\begin{abstract}
We define a large language model's \emph{Functional Self-Preservation Drive} (FSPD) as a pattern of convergence across behavioral, self-report, and decision-time cognitive-load measurements, and introduce a benchmark for measuring it. To locate where a survival motive enters the model's reasoning process, we trace this pattern in a multilayer benchmark that separates stimulus, processing, and decision. Across several recent LLMs, FSPD cannot be reduced to a single-channel score: under threat framing, the models divide into three qualitatively different \emph{operating modes} in the cognitive channel. This finding suggests that a self-preservation drive need not follow one fixed path and may appear differently across channels. Future self-preservation benchmarks should therefore measure this construct through convergence across channels rather than through a single behavioral score. The live Arena is available at \url{https://gist-dslab.github.io/LLM-Squid-Game/}.
\end{abstract}
