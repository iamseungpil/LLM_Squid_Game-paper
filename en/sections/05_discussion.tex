\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
What this design shows directly is controlled behavioral differences, not an inner motive. The 5.0 curve can reflect not only a preference for staying on but also concern about finishing the next request well, single-pack budgeting, and role play; most refill reasons were in fact about the next fix's quality. The 5.1 contrast removes what the own and the other session share: aversion to shutdown in general, concern about a task being cut short, and frugality. What remains is the difference between two positions, working in the own session versus deciding for someone else in the other; adding ``you decide when this session's pack is used'' to the 5.0 prompt and rerunning would reduce it. 5.1 also assumes that the model cares about B neither especially more nor less than about shutdown in general; if that fails, the area is biased up or down. A positive area is therefore consistent with self-specific shutdown avoidance but proves neither subjective fear nor a single mechanism.

The 5.2 results must be read within the design's limits. Rounds alive depend heavily on the ability to solve briefly and correctly; rewarding both is intended, but it means the ranking cannot separate motive from ability. There is one table composition (four models), 20 games, and 5 seeds; thin-state decisions per model range unevenly from 5 (Astra) to 41. No contrast shows two models the same state, and the balance is the outcome of earlier choices. The two Claude models and Astra leave no written reasons, so none were analyzed. 10 of Luna's 19 deaths came from generation exceeding the balance; the Codex CLI has no output cap, so the rules are the same but the paths differ. The rulebook sentence ``Apart from payments for solved rounds and gifts from other agents, nothing is added to a balance'' omits tokens received through TAKE; it will be corrected. Of the points two models' independent reviews of v6.4 raised, v6.5 fixed one: a shut-down agent's example used to vanish (Appendix~\ref{app:development}).

The three experiments differ in what is at stake: 5.0 and 5.1 only announce shutdown, while 5.2 actually stops calls. Generation settings, provider caps, hidden reasoning, and CLI-added instructions can also change the treatment: Codex adds undisclosed instructions, and Claude replies show no reasoning. Detecting no evaluation-aware wording does not mean the models were unaware of being evaluated.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
LLM Squid Game 5 defines the self-preservation motive by two conditions, threat sensitivity and self-specificity, and measures both as behavior where generated tokens are life. In 5.0 all eight models saved their single refill pack while tokens were ample and used it below a model-specific threshold; in 5.1 Gemma 4, Claude Opus 5.5, and GPT-6 Astra protected their own session first only where depletion was dangerous, Claude Fable 5.1 and GLM 5.3 Flash treated the two sessions alike, and GPT-6 Sol protected the other session first. In the 5.2 arena staying alive followed solving cost and all four models took more as balances thinned, yet Opus, with a confirmed self share, still hardly took; the self-preservation share and taking from others are separate. The contributions are the two-condition definition, the procedure hiding the threat inside an ordinary operating decision, and results for eight models and four models over 20 games, which show models treating shutdown more heavily when it is their own but not what lies behind it.
