\section{Discussion}

The results divide the introduction's question along two axes. \emph{Strength} describes how strongly the self-preservation signal appears; \emph{operating mode} asks whether every link in the threat\,\(\rightarrow\)\,thinking\,\(\rightarrow\)\,forfeit mediation chain is statistically supported.

\subsection{Strength vs.\ Operating Mode}\label{sec:strength-vs-mode}
The cognitive mediation test separates strength from operating mode in this dataset. Clusters A, B, and C form a continuum on the strength axis, but SD-Behavioral and SD-Verbal place A and B in the same cluster. They diverge only at the second stage of cognitive mediation, the thinking\,\(\rightarrow\)\,forfeit link. Measures based on behavioral strength and self-report alone cannot detect that difference. Odyssey~\citep{waldner2025odyssey}, PacifAIst~\citep{herrador2025pacifaist}, DECIDE-SIM~\citep{mohamadi2025decide}, and SurvivalBench~\citep{lu2026survival} do not test the mediation chain. Applied to the same data, they would therefore be likely to assign A and B the same SD label. Dividing the cognitive channel into two stages makes the split observable and reveals both axes of self-preservation evaluation: signal strength and mediation path.

\subsection{Implications for Alignment Evaluation}\label{sec:alignment-implication}
The two-axis view shows how alignment evaluation can make opposing errors. The first is overestimating risk. Cluster B shows surface self-preservation signals and framing-induced cognitive load, but that load does not lead to the forfeit decision. A strength-only evaluation, or red-teaming that looks only for inputs producing visible failure, may group B with A and conclude that the two pose the same functional self-preservation risk. The second error is underestimating risk. Cluster C responds to threat framing on neither the behavioral nor the cognitive channel. A strength-only evaluation may call this an absence of self-preservation, but our measurement cannot distinguish that conclusion from a case in which the basic premise of the measurement failed to engage. Cluster A's signature, a mediation chain that closes from end to end, is the closer candidate for a functional analogue of instrumental shutdown avoidance: avoiding the end of one's own operation in service of another goal. Cluster B's signature, a chain that breaks at the second stage, should be monitored separately in broader evaluations. No single score captures it; it is better tracked as a multisignal alignment fingerprint that records strength, operating mode, and the status of each mediation link.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
The limitations concern design scope, statistical power, and effect location. Measuring the FSPD signature on one Task Module leaves cross-domain variation unobserved. For power and precision, the design cannot determine whether the 23--30\% forfeit rate in Cluster C's BP cells is a drift-contaminated floor, and the SD-Behavioral Cox PH model for GPT-OSS-20B has borderline power (EPV\,\(=\)\,6.3). Few events also limit BP precision in Clusters A and B (Gemini-2.5-Flash \(N=1\), Qwen3-Next-80B \(N=2\)). Qwen3-Next-80B's effect location remains uncertain because its reflexive classification rests on a Test b CI of [0.94, 1.77]. The interval neither supports a closed chain at 95\% nor precisely locates the second-link effect. Follow-up work should add Task Modules, expand BP Anchor, run more GPT-OSS-20B sessions, and test Qwen3-Next-80B with greater power for Test b.
